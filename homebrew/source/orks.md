# Orks for The Horus Heresy 2.0 — Homebrew Conversion

2026-10-04 · @Isaac

## Introduction

This is a homebrew conversion of the full 7th edition Codex: Orks (2014) range for The Horus Heresy 2.0 (the 2022 rulebook). It uses 2.0 mechanics only: no Damage characteristic, no Challenge sub-phase, Gambits or Focus rolls, no Tactical Statuses, and no 3.0 army selection. Challenges work the 2.0 way, as in 7th.

It also adds Ork units introduced in 8th through 11th edition 40k, such as the Beast Snaggas, Speed Freek buggies and the Big Mek Dakkarig. Each slot section lists these after the 7th edition units.

The design goals are simple. Orks win through numbers and assault. Their shooting is unreliable (BS2). Small mobs break easily, big mobs hold. Vehicles are cheap and fragile but hard to kill outright.

Build armies with the HH2.0 Crusade Force Organisation chart, using the slot given for each unit. Points start from 7th edition costs and are adjusted where 2.0 changes a unit's value; treat them as playtest values. Rules marked † lean on exact 2.0 wording and should be checked against the rulebook; the checklist near the end of the document lists them all.

## Army-wide Special Rules

Every unit in this list has **'Ere We Go!** and **Waaagh!** unless its entry says otherwise. **Mob Rule** goes to the units its own rule names. Vehicles have **Ramshackled Vehicle** (X), and the mechanised buggies have **Ramshackled Carapace** (X). Knights and Titans have (5+); all other Ork vehicles and buggies have (6+).

**Ammo Runts.** Ammo Runts scurry alongside their masters carrying spare ammunition, replacement magazines and unstable experimental rounds looted from countless battlefields.

Once per battle, before making a Shooting Attack, a model accompanied by one or more Ammo Runts may re-roll all failed To Hit rolls and failed To Wound rolls for a single Shooting Attack.

After the attack has been resolved, remove one Ammo Runt from play.

**Bigga iz Betta.** Models with the Bulky (X) special rule that join a unit add X to the total number of models in the unit for the purposes of determining Mob Rule bonuses.

In addition, Cavalry models count as four models each for the purposes of determining Mob Rule bonuses.

**Breakin' Heads.** If a unit with Mob Rule that includes a Nob, Boss Nob or Ork character fails a Morale check or Pinning test, it may choose to pass it instead. If it does, the unit suffers D3 automatic hits at Strength 6, AP –, allocated by the Ork player to models other than characters; armour saves may be taken.

**Da Biggest is Da Best.** Among the Orks, leadership belongs only to the strongest, loudest and most violently successful warlords. The greatest bosses dominate their mobs through brute strength, terrifying aggression and the simple certainty that anyone challenging their authority will be beaten to death publicly and immediately. Entire Waaagh!s rise and fall around such figures as lesser Orks instinctively gather beneath the biggest and meanest leaders they can find.

A model with the Da Biggest is Da Best special rule grants the following benefits:

- **Waaagh! Leader:** If a Detachment contains at least one model with this special rule, that Detachment may select a single Waaagh! Doctrine or equivalent army-wide Ork upgrade if permitted by the campaign organizer.
- **The Biggest and the Meanest:** An army may only include a maximum of one model with this special rule per 1,000 points.
- **Mob Boss:** A model with this special rule may select an eligible Retinue or Nob bodyguard unit as part of the same Force Organisation slot.

Da Biggest is Da Best is treated as equivalent to Master of the Legion for the purposes of any campaign rules, mission effects or special interactions that reference Master of the Legion.

**Dakka! (X).** When making Shooting Attacks with Auto weapons, unmodified To Hit rolls of X+ generate one additional hit.

Additional hits generated in this way do not themselves generate additional hits.

**Deadly Demise.** When a model with this special rule is destroyed, but before it is removed from play, all units within 6" suffer a Strength 8 AP – hit with Rending (6+).

**Deck Crew (X).** This weapon can only be used if the vehicle is locked in combat or has been charged. In the Initiative 1 step of the Fight sub-phase, the vehicle model makes a fixed number of attacks equal to X using this profile. These attacks hit automatically if the vehicle is stationary, or hit on a 4+ if the vehicle moved in the preceding Movement phase. These attacks do not benefit from charging or any army-wide special rules.

**Dread Mob.** When deployed onto the battlefield, either at the start of the battle or when arriving from Reserves, all models with this special rule in the same unit must be deployed within unit coherency (3").

After deployment, all models immediately operate independently and are no longer treated as a single unit.

**'Ere We Go!** When rolling charge distance, the unit may re-roll one of the dice. If the charge still fails, the unit moves the full distance rolled toward the target, instead of the usual reduced failed-charge move†.

**Exhaust Belch.** After this unit completes a Run move, place a flamer template touching the rear of the model. Any unit touched by the template suffers D3 Strength 5 AP – hits with the Pinning special rule.

**Fetishes of Gork and Mork.** Fetishes of Gork and Mork grant a 4+ Invulnerable Save and Adamantium Will (4+).

**Grot Attendants.** Grot Attendants are grots that are attached to other units. They provide a special bonus to the mob while they are still alive. In addition, they are all armed with a grot blasta and grot stabba unless otherwise noted.

A model with this rule does not count towards the unit's size, never gains Klan (X), and may not issue, accept or be chosen for a challenge. Hits can only be allocated to it by attacks with Precision Shots or Precision Strikes. When a Wound would be allocated to another model in its unit, the controlling player may allocate that Wound to a model with this rule instead. If it is removed, any bonus it gives is lost.

**Grot Orderly.** Grot Orderlies are terrified little surgical assistants, pain suppressors and battlefield medics that follow their masters carrying crude stimulants, spare body parts and improvised surgical tools.

A model accompanied by one or more Grot Orderlies gains Feel No Pain (6+).

If the model already possesses Feel No Pain, improve that roll by +1 to a maximum of Feel No Pain (4+).

When a model benefiting from a Grot Orderly fails a Feel No Pain roll, the controlling player may instead remove one Grot Orderly from play. The Grot Orderly suffers the wound instead of the targeted model.

**Grot Piloted.** This model may use the Leadership characteristic of the nearest friendly Ork Infantry Character within 12" when making Morale checks and Pinning checks.

If no qualifying model is within range, this model suffers −1 Weapon Skill.

**Grot Riggers (X).** At the end of each of your turns, roll a D6 for each model with this rule that has lost Hull Points. On a roll of X or more, it restores one lost Hull Point, up to its starting value. A model cannot use Grot Riggers in a turn it was embarked, Falling Back or locked in combat†.

**Kommando Grot.** Kommando Grots are sneaky little saboteurs, ammunition carriers and lookouts that creep ahead of the main mob marking targets, planting charges and slipping through spaces too small for larger Orks.

A unit containing a Kommando Grot gains +1 to all Cover Saves while wholly within terrain.

In addition, a unit containing a Kommando Grot gains Precision Shots (6+) during the first turn of the game.

**Luck of Mork and Gork.** Gretchin units gain a 6+ Invulnerable Save against attacks made with Blast or Template weapons.

**Mek-Smasha (X).** If a model with the Mek-Smasha (X) special rule is in base contact with, or Embarked upon, one or more damaged Vehicles, Dreadnoughts, Automata, or Mechanized units during the Shooting phase, they can attempt to repair one of them. If they do so, the model that attempted the repair cannot shoot any weapons or use any other abilities that would be used instead of making a Shooting Attack.

Roll a D6. If the result is equal to or more than the value listed in brackets as part of this rule, the repair is successful. Apply one of the following options to the target unit the model is in base contact with or Embarked upon:

- Repair a Weapon Destroyed result.
- Repair an Immobilised result.
- Restore a lost Hull Point.
- Restore a lost Wound.

If a Weapon Destroyed result is repaired, that weapon may not be used to attack in the same phase as it is repaired, but may be used to attack as normal in any phase after that. The Mek-Smasha cannot use this ability if they are Pinned or Falling Back.

**Might iz Right!** Ork units with the Bulky (X) special rule add X to their Initiative Characteristic while engaged in a Challenge, to a maximum Initiative Characteristic of 7.

**Mob Rule.** Any Ork Infantry, Jump Infantry or Cavalry unit that is not a Walker, Grot or Vehicle gains the Mob Rule special rule.

- If a unit with this special rule contains 10 or more models, it gains Stubborn.
- If a unit with this special rule contains 20 or more models, it gains Fearless and Rage (2).
- If a unit with this special rule contains 30 or more models, it gains Fearless and Rage (3).

**Mob Up.** Two Ork units of the same type that end their Movement phase within 2" of each other may attempt to Mob Up.

To Mob Up, the controlling player must immediately take a Leadership test using the highest Leadership characteristic available between the two units.

If the test is passed, the two units immediately merge into a single unit for the remainder of the battle.

If the test is failed, both units immediately suffer D6 Strength 6 AP – hits that also inflict Pinning.

Only units of the same type may Mob Up. Shoota Boyz may Mob Up only with other Shoota Boyz mobs.

**Mo' Dakka.** When a model with this special rule generates an additional hit from Dakka!, the additional hit gains all of the same special rules and effects possessed by the original attack, including Rending, Breaching, Armourbane, Pinning and similar weapon effects.

**Oddboy.** Oddboyz may join Ork units normally, but do not count toward unit size when determining bonuses granted by the Mob Rule special rule.

**Oil Runts.** Oil Runts are filthy little mechanics and lubricant carriers that swarm over weapons, armour plates and damaged machinery attempting crude battlefield repairs while avoiding being stepped on by their larger kin.

Once per battle, at the start of the controlling player's turn, a model accompanied by one or more Oil Runts may immediately regain a single lost Wound or Hull Point.

After the repair has been resolved, remove one Oil Runt from play.

**Open-topped.** The vehicle gains the Assault Vehicle special rule, but adds 1 to its rolls on the Vehicle Damage table when it suffers a penetrating hit. The entire vehicle counts as its Access Point.

**Ramshackled Carapace (X).** This unit is a ramshackle mechanical nightmare held together through crude reinforcement, unstable reactors and the barely competent maintenance of terrified grot crews. Though wildly unreliable, these clanking engines of war are often far harder to destroy than logic would suggest.

A model with the Ramshackled Carapace (X) special rule gains an X+ Invulnerable Save.

In addition, a model with Ramshackled Carapace (X) and a Wounds Characteristic that suffers an unsaved Wound with the Instant Death special rule is not immediately removed as a casualty, but instead loses D3 Wounds for each unsaved Wound inflicted by the attack.

When a model with Ramshackled Carapace (X) loses its last Wound, but before it is removed as a casualty, all models, both friendly and enemy, within D6" suffer an automatic Strength 8 AP – hit.

**Ramshackled Vehicle (X).** Ork vehicles are infamous for being impossible creations of crude engineering, scavenged armour plating and dangerously unstable mechanical improvisation. Built from stolen hulls, battlefield wreckage and whatever scrap metal happened to survive the last explosion, these ramshackle machines should barely function at all. Yet through some combination of brutal overengineering, improbable luck and the inexplicable genius of the Ork mekboyz, many continue fighting long after more sophisticated war machines would have been destroyed.

A vehicle with the Ramshackled Vehicle (X) special rule gains an X+ Invulnerable Save.

In addition, when a vehicle with the Ramshackled Vehicle (X) special rule suffers a Penetrating Hit, roll a D6. On a result of X or greater, the Penetrating Hit is instead treated as a Glancing Hit. However, on a roll of 1, add +2 to the roll made on the Vehicle Damage table.

**Retinue.** A Warboss Retinue may be selected as a Retinue for a Warboss. While joined by the Warboss for whom it was selected as a Retinue:

- the unit does not use up an additional Force Organisation slot
- all models in the unit gain the Line Sub-type

If the attached Warboss is removed as a casualty, the unit immediately loses the Line Sub-type unless another rule would grant it.

**Runt Orkalytes.** At the beginning of any phase, the controlling player may remove one Runt Orkalyte from play. If they do, the Weirdboy immediately gains +1 Waaagh! Energy until the end of the current turn.

If a Runt Orkalyte is removed as a casualty by a Precision Shots or Precision Strikes attack, all units within D6" suffer D3+1 hits at Strength D3+1 and AP D6+1.

**Stealth.** If the model has not moved this turn, it cannot be targeted by ranged attacks from more than 12" away.

**Speed Freekz.** Models with this special rule gain Fleet (2), Sudden Strike (2), and Hammer of Wrath (1). In addition, they may re-roll failed Dangerous Terrain tests.

**Tank Hunters.** The model's attacks gain the Sunder special rule. If a weapon already has Sunder, add 1 to its Armour Penetration rolls instead.

**Titanic Presence.** Friendly Ork Infantry, Cavalry and Jump Infantry units within 18" of a Stompa gain Stubborn.

In addition, friendly Ork units within 18" may use the Stompa's Leadership characteristic of 10 when making Morale checks and Pinning checks.

A Stompa also counts as 15 additional models for any special rule or ability that counts the number of nearby Ork models.

**Waaagh!** Once per game, at the start of your turn, if your Warlord is an Ork and is on the table, you may call a Waaagh! Until the end of that turn, every unit with this rule gains the following:

- **Fleet (+1):** the unit gains Fleet (1), or increases an existing Fleet (X) by 1.
- **Furious Charge (+1):** the unit gains Furious Charge (1), or increases an existing Furious Charge (X) by 1.

Units with Mob Rule also automatically pass Morale checks until the start of your next turn.

## The Waaagh!

The Waaagh! is not merely an army, nor even a species-wide instinct for war. It is a psychic phenomenon generated by the collective presence of the Orks themselves, growing larger and more violent as more greenskins gather together for battle. Wherever Ork warbands converge in sufficient numbers, the atmosphere itself seems to thicken with aggression and anticipation. Tempers flare, engines roar louder, weapons malfunction less frequently and even the weakest grot somehow finds the courage to charge directly into gunfire beside his larger kin. To Imperial scholars and Navigators, the Waaagh! manifests as a crude but unmistakable psychic pressure spreading outward from major Ork concentrations like a storm front of violence and instinct.

Unlike the carefully controlled psychic arts of human psykers, the Waaagh! is entirely unconscious. Most Orks possess no understanding whatsoever of the power they generate together, nor would they care if they did. To them, battle simply feels right. The larger the mob grows, the more reality itself bends subtly around their expectations. Engines somehow continue running despite catastrophic damage. Ammunition feeds never seem to empty at the correct moment. Ramshackle war machines surge forward through sheer momentum and improbable luck long after logic insists they should have exploded. Entire Mek workshops produce impossible machines through instinct and collective psychic reinforcement rather than true engineering.

As a Waaagh! expands, its effects become increasingly visible and dangerous. Small warbands may generate only minor battlefield phenomena such as heightened aggression or mechanical resilience, but massive Waaagh!s can destabilize entire systems. Warp routes become erratic, astropathic communication collapses and nearby psykers often report overwhelming psychic noise resembling endless screaming, laughter and gunfire echoing through the Immaterium itself. Weirdboyz become particularly dangerous within large Waaagh!s, acting as living conduits for the accumulating psychic pressure until they either unleash catastrophic displays of power or explode violently beneath the strain.

To the Imperium, the true horror of the Waaagh! is not simply its violence, but its momentum. Every victory strengthens it. Every conquered world feeds it. Defeated enemies provide weapons, vehicles, slaves and raw materials for the next stage of expansion while surviving Orks attract more warbands from across nearby systems. What begins as isolated raids or scattered pirate attacks may rapidly evolve into a sector-wide apocalypse once enough greenskins gather beneath a powerful warlord. Entire Imperial campaigns have collapsed because commanders underestimated how quickly a growing Waaagh! can transform from a localized threat into a civilization-consuming war machine.

At the height of a true Waaagh!, the distinction between military campaign, migration and natural disaster effectively disappears. Countless warbands merge together beneath the largest and strongest bosses while Titans, battlefleets and hive worlds vanish beneath endless tides of green bodies and roaring engines. Even the Traitor Legions and the servants of Chaos have learned to fear such events, for the Waaagh! does not negotiate, retreat or truly end while enemies remain standing. It simply grows larger, louder and more violent until either the galaxy breaks beneath it or the Orks finally find something worthy of fighting.

## Waaagh! Doctrine

The Waaagh! is more than merely the war cry of the Orks. It is a psychic phenomenon generated by the collective aggression, violence and momentum of countless greenskins gathered together for battle. As Ork armies grow larger and the fighting intensifies, this latent psychic pressure spreads throughout nearby mobs, war machines and Weirdboyz alike, amplifying the destructive instincts of the entire horde. Though few Orks understand the true nature of the Waaagh!, every warband instinctively channels its energy toward the forms of warfare it values most.

Different warbands focus this psychic momentum in radically different ways. Some hurl themselves into direct melee slaughter amidst screaming charges and brutal close combat, while others unleash impossible storms of gunfire or roaring mechanized assaults driven by the momentum of the Waaagh! itself. Amongst the strangest warbands, Weirdboyz and Warpheads manipulate the psychic energy directly, transforming the battlefield into a catastrophic storm of green lightning, exploding reality and uncontrolled Warp manifestations. These battlefield philosophies become known amongst the Orks as Waaagh! Doctrines.

A Waaagh! Doctrine represents the dominant expression of the psychic momentum surrounding an Ork army once battle reaches its peak. Whether through overwhelming violence, impossible speed, endless gunfire or catastrophic psychic power, the Waaagh! focuses itself through the collective instincts of the horde until the entire battlefield descends into barely controlled chaos. Once unleashed, the Waaagh! transforms the Ork warbands from dangerous raiders into an unstoppable tide of destruction capable of overwhelming even the greatest armies of the Imperium.

A Detachment that contains at least one model with the Da Biggest is Da Best special rule may select a single Waaagh! Doctrine. A Waaagh! Doctrine is unleashed when the army calls its Waaagh!: it counts as the army's one Waaagh! call for the battle, and its effects apply along with those of the Waaagh! special rule.

### Krumpin' Waaagh!

_The Krumpin' Waaagh! is the purest expression of Ork brutality unleashed across the battlefield. As the roar of the Waaagh! spreads through the mobs, every Ork surges forward in a tide of violence and momentum that few enemies can survive. Infantry mobs charge headlong into enemy lines while Dreadnoughts and armoured brutes smash through fortifications and infantry alike beneath a storm of gunfire, blades and roaring engines. During a Krumpin' Waaagh!, discipline and caution vanish entirely as the Orks become consumed by the simple joy of close-range slaughter._

_To Imperial observers, a Krumpin' Waaagh! resembles less a coordinated military assault and more a natural disaster. Entire front lines collapse beneath the weight of charging greenskins while wounded Orks continue fighting through injuries that should have killed them outright. The psychic momentum generated by the Waaagh! itself drives the Orks into an almost unstoppable frenzy of aggression and destruction._

Once per battle, at the start of any turn, all friendly Ork Infantry and Dreadnought units gain:

- Fleet (2)
- Furious Charge (2)
- Fearless
- Feel No Pain (5+)
- Dakka! (5+)

The effect lasts until the end of the turn.

### Speed Waaagh!

_The Speed Waaagh! represents the war doctrines of the Speed Freeks, biker mobs and fast-moving raider warbands that sweep across the battlefield with terrifying speed. Engines scream, exhausts belch fire and entire formations surge forward in clouds of smoke and burning fuel as Warbikes, buggies, jump infantry and ramshackle vehicles hurl themselves into battle. During a Speed Waaagh!, the battlefield becomes a blur of motion where even the largest Ork vehicles move with reckless velocity._

_To the Orks, speed itself is a weapon. Fast-moving assaults crash into enemy formations before defenses can react while reserve forces surge unexpectedly into battle amidst clouds of smoke and roaring engines. Entire armoured columns may disappear into dust and debris only to reappear moments later smashing directly into vulnerable enemy positions. The Speed Waaagh! transforms the normally chaotic momentum of the Orks into something terrifyingly mobile and unpredictable._

Once per battle, at the start of any turn, all friendly Ork Cavalry, Jump Infantry, Skirmish and Vehicle units gain:

- Fleet (3)
- Sudden Strike (3)
- Hammer of Wrath (+1)
- Shrouded (5+) while Running

In addition, all friendly Ork units gain +2 to Reserve rolls during this turn.

The effect lasts until the end of the turn.

### Dakka Waaagh!

_The Dakka Waaagh! is the culmination of every Ork belief that no problem exists which cannot be solved through overwhelming quantities of gunfire. During a Dakka Waaagh!, the battlefield vanishes beneath torrents of shells, tracer fire and ricocheting scrap metal as entire mobs unleash impossible volumes of shooting without concern for ammunition conservation, overheating or basic mechanical safety. Big Shootas roar continuously, Deffguns shake themselves apart under sustained fire and even the largest Ork war engines become mobile storms of bullets and explosions._

_Though wildly inaccurate by Imperial standards, the sheer density of fire unleashed during a Dakka Waaagh! is often enough to overwhelm even elite enemy formations through suppression and attrition alone. Entire sectors of the battlefield become impassable beneath the storm of incoming fire while enemy infantry are pinned beneath the relentless noise and violence of the advancing greenskins. The Dakka Waaagh! is not precision warfare. It is destruction through excess._

Once per battle, at the start of any turn, all friendly Ork units gain:

- Dakka! (4+)
- Twin-linked on all Auto weapons
- Pinning on all Auto weapons
- Breaching (6+) on all Auto weapons
- Relentless

In addition, Heavy weapons fired by friendly Ork units gain +1 Shot, to a maximum increase of +3 shots per weapon.

The effect lasts until the end of the turn.

### Odd Waaagh!

_The Odd Waaagh! represents the terrifying moment when the psychic energy of the Waaagh! becomes concentrated around Weirdboyz, Warpheads and other unstable Ork psykers. As the battle intensifies, the raw psychic pressure generated by thousands of Orks begins boiling over into catastrophic Warp manifestations and impossible surges of power. Weirdboyz become living conduits for the Waaagh! itself, channeling destructive psychic energy across the battlefield with little understanding or control over what they unleash._

_To nearby Orks, the presence of an empowered Weirdboy fills them with violent excitement and reckless confidence. Entire mobs charge screaming into battle beneath storms of green lightning and exploding reality while the Weirdboyz themselves teeter constantly upon the edge of catastrophic psychic collapse. Even the Orks rarely understand exactly what an Odd Waaagh! will do once unleashed — only that it will be loud, dangerous and very likely hilarious right up until something explodes._

Once per battle, at the start of any turn, all friendly Weirdboyz and Psykers gain:

- +2 Leadership for Psychic checks
- Adamantium Will (4+)
- Feel No Pain (4+)

All friendly Ork units within 12" of a friendly Weirdboy or Weirdboss gain:

- Counter-Attack (2)
- Furious Charge (2)

In addition, all Weirdboy Psychic Weapons gain Rending (5+) during this turn.

The effect lasts until the end of the turn.

## Advanced Reaction: Get Stuck In

Orks use the 2.0 core reactions (Reposition, Return Fire, Overwatch) as normal, plus this advanced reaction. It costs one point of the army's Reaction Allotment† like any other reaction.

**Get Stuck In** (Movement phase). Trigger: an enemy unit ends a move within 12" of, and in line of sight to, an Ork Infantry or Cavalry unit. The reacting unit immediately moves up to D6 plus its Initiative Characteristic in inches, directly toward the triggering unit, and must end closer to it than it started. If this move ends with the reacting unit engaged with the triggering unit, the reacting unit counts as having charged it, gaining any bonuses for charging, and the combat is fought in the next Assault phase†. The reaction may not be used by a unit that is Pinned, Falling Back, embarked, or already locked in combat.

The point of this reaction is to punish an opponent who stops near a mob of Orks: if they come too close, the Orks crash into them on their own terms.

## Psychic Discipline: Power of da Waaagh!

There is no psychic phase in 2.0. A Weirdboy uses his powers in the phase each one names, making a Psychic check against his Leadership†. A failed check causes Perils of the Waaagh!. Ork Psychic checks also suffer Perils on any double, as described under Waaagh! Energy.

**Waaagh! Energy.** A Weirdboy increases its Leadership characteristic by +1 for the purposes of making Psychic checks for every 10 friendly Ork Infantry models within 12", to a maximum bonus of +3. His Leadership can never be raised above 10 by this rule. Models with both the Bigga iz Betta and Bulky (X) special rules count as X additional models for the purposes of determining Waaagh! Energy. The power of the Waaagh! is hard to control: an Ork Psychic check suffers Perils of the Warp on any double, even if the check is passed.

**Perils of the Waaagh!** If a Weirdboy fails a Psychic check, it automatically suffers Perils of the Warp. In addition, all units within D6" plus the current Waaagh! Energy bonus suffer D6 Strength 3 AP – hits with the Pinning and Shell Shock (1) special rules. The Weirdboy's current Waaagh! Energy bonus is also added to:

- the range of the effect
- the Strength of the hits
- the number of hits inflicted
- the value of the Shell Shock special rule

Every Weirdboy knows Da Jump and Frazzle. A Weirdboy with the Warphead upgrade also knows Fists of Gork.

Before deployment, roll one D3 on the Weirdboy Psychic Powers table and one D3 on the Weirdboy Psychic Weapons table. The Weirdboy gains the generated power and weapon for the duration of the battle.

**Psychic Focus.** Before making any To Hit rolls with a Psychic Weapon, the Weirdboy must make a Psychic check. If the check is passed, the Weirdboy may attack normally with the selected weapon. If the check is failed:

- the Weirdboy suffers Perils of the Warp
- and may not use the Psychic Weapon during that phase.

**Shock Pulse.** Any unit that suffers one or more unsaved Wounds from this weapon suffers −1 WS until the end of the next Assault phase.

## Ork Klans

Every Ork belongs to a Klan, and each Klan fights in its own way. Klans work like the Legiones Astartes (X) special rule: they give an army its character without changing its datasheets.

**Klan (X).** When building an army, choose one Klan for its Primary Detachment; an Allied Detachment may choose a different Klan. Every unit in that detachment with the Mob Rule special rule, and every Ork vehicle and Dreadnought, gains Klan (X), where X is the chosen Klan. Gretchin units and Gretchin crew never gain Klan (X).

**Klan Advanced Reactions.** Each Klan has its own Advanced Reaction, usable only by units with that Klan (X) rule. It is available in addition to Get Stuck In and costs one point of the Reaction Allotment like any other reaction†.

**Named characters.** A Unique character belongs to the Klan listed below and may only be included in a detachment of that Klan. Kaptin Badrukk is the exception: he may be included in a Bad Moons or a Freebooterz detachment, and takes that detachment's Klan.

## Units

Ork warfare is built around momentum, aggression and the overwhelming psychic force of the Waaagh!. Unlike the rigid military doctrines of the Imperium, greenskin armies fight as living avalanches of violence that grow more dangerous the larger and louder they become. Individual units rarely operate with formal battlefield discipline, yet together they generate a terrifying instinctive coordination driven by the momentum of battle itself. Every gunshot, engine roar and screaming charge feeds the collective fury of the Waaagh! and pushes the entire horde toward ever greater destruction.

At the core of nearly every warband stand the Boyz mobs, vast tides of greenskin infantry armed with crude shootas, choppas and endless enthusiasm for close combat. Boyz fight through sheer aggression rather than tactical sophistication, hurling themselves into enemy lines with overwhelming confidence regardless of casualties. Even when disorganized, large mobs of Boyz become terrifying through sheer mass alone, drowning disciplined formations beneath endless waves of roaring greenskins hacking wildly with blades and firing inaccurate torrents of gunfire at point-blank range.

Shoota Boyz represent the most common expression of Ork firepower, advancing steadily beneath storms of suppressive dakka before crashing into close combat once enemy lines begin collapsing. To Imperial tacticians, their shooting appears wildly inaccurate and poorly coordinated, yet the sheer volume of fire generated by a large mob often compensates for the greenskins' lack of precision. Entire sectors of the battlefield may vanish beneath shell casings, smoke and ricocheting scrap metal as the Boyz surge forward through the chaos they create themselves.

The elite Nobz and Warboss retinues embody the Ork obsession with size and dominance. Massive even by greenskin standards, these brutal veterans lead through physical violence and overwhelming personal power. Clad in thick armour and wielding enormous power klaws, killsaws and oversized choppas, Nobz smash through elite infantry and armoured formations alike with terrifying ease. Wherever the fighting becomes bloodiest, the Nobz inevitably gather there, seeking the loudest and most violent battles available.

Meganobz and heavily armoured assault formations transform this brutality into something even more horrifying. Encased in massive suits of Mega Armour that resemble walking tanks more than infantry, these Orks advance through incoming fire with relentless momentum while unleashing torrents of gunfire and crushing melee attacks at close range. Entire Imperial defensive lines have collapsed simply from the psychological shock of watching armoured greenskins continue advancing through firepower that should have annihilated them outright.

The speed-obsessed cultures of the Evil Sunz and Speed Freeks fight entirely differently from traditional infantry mobs. Warbikers, trukks, buggies and roaring assault vehicles race across battlefields in clouds of smoke and burning fuel while jump infantry descend from above amidst screaming engines and unstable rockets. To these greenskins, speed itself is a weapon. Their assaults rely upon momentum, confusion and overwhelming velocity to strike vulnerable enemy positions before organized resistance can form.

Other Ork formations specialize in sheer firepower. Lootas, Flash Gitz and heavy weapon mobs unleash staggering quantities of dakka using scavenged Deffguns, Snazzguns and oversized artillery pieces assembled from stolen machinery and unstable reactor systems. Though wildly dangerous to friend and foe alike, these weapons can overwhelm even elite armoured formations through sheer volume of fire. Entire firefights involving Ork heavy weapon mobs often resemble industrial disasters more than organized military engagements.

Ork walkers and war engines reflect the deranged creativity of the mekboyz that build them. Killa Kans, Deff Dreads and towering Stompas lumber across battlefields belching smoke, leaking fuel and shaking apart beneath the stress of their own engines. Yet despite their crude appearance, these machines possess terrifying durability and destructive power. Many Imperial commanders have learned too late that the ramshackle appearance of Ork war machines hides an astonishing ability to absorb punishment while continuing forward through sheer mechanical stubbornness.

**Squig Prodder.** A Squig Prodder is a vicious shock stave used to drive attack squigs and terrified Gretchin into uncontrollable aggression. Friendly Gretchin units within 6" of one or more models equipped with a squig prodder gain Furious Charge (1).

**Killsaw.** A model equipped with two Killsaws gains +2 Attacks instead of the normal +1 Attack granted for being armed with two close combat weapons.

Grots never gain +1 Attack for being armed with two melee weapons.

### Squig Jaws

_The mounts of squig-riding Orks bite just as hard as their riders hit._

Squig jaws and similar mount weapons have no weapon profile. Instead, a model with one gains Hammer of Wrath (X), where X is the number of Attacks shown below; those Hammer of Wrath hits use the Strength shown. If the model already has Hammer of Wrath (X) from another rule, use whichever value is higher.
